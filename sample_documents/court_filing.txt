STATEMENT OF FACTS

1. On or about June 3, 2022, Plaintiff Metropolitan Transit Authority
("MTA") entered into Contract No. MTA-2022-0789 with Defendant TriState
Construction Group, Inc. ("TriState") for the renovation of the Grand
Central Terminal underground concourse (the "Project").

2. The total contract value was Twenty-Seven Million Dollars ($27,000,000.00),
with an expected completion date of December 31, 2023.

3. TriState commenced work on August 1, 2022, and was required to maintain
a construction schedule approved by MTA's Project Manager, James T.
Richardson.

4. Between September 2022 and March 2023, TriState submitted seven (7)
change order requests totaling $4,200,000.00. MTA approved three (3)
change orders totaling $1,850,000.00 and denied four (4) change orders
totaling $2,350,000.00.

5. On April 15, 2023, MTA's independent inspector, Chen Engineering
Associates, issued a deficiency report identifying thirty-two (32)
instances of non-conforming work, including:
   (a) Improper installation of fire-rated wall assemblies in Zones B
       and D;
   (b) Failure to meet ADA accessibility requirements at six (6)
       passenger access points;
   (c) Use of non-specified concrete mix in structural support columns.

6. Despite receiving written notice of these deficiencies on April 20, 2023
(MTA Letter No. 2023-0420), TriState failed to cure the deficiencies
within the sixty (60) day cure period specified in Section 12.3 of the
Contract.

7. On July 1, 2023, MTA issued a Notice of Default (MTA Letter No.
2023-0701) and subsequently terminated the Contract for cause on
August 15, 2023.

8. As of the date of termination, only 47% of the Project work had been
completed, and MTA had made progress payments totaling $14,300,000.00
to TriState.

9. MTA subsequently entered into a replacement contract with Atlas
Infrastructure Corp. for $18,500,000.00 to complete the remaining work.

ARGUMENT

I. TRISTATE BREACHED THE CONTRACT BY FAILING TO PERFORM CONFORMING WORK

TriState's failure to perform work in accordance with the Contract
specifications constitutes a material breach. The thirty-two (32)
deficiencies identified by Chen Engineering Associates demonstrate a
pattern of non-conforming work that goes to the essence of the Contract.

II. MTA PROPERLY TERMINATED THE CONTRACT FOR CAUSE

Section 12.3 of the Contract provides that MTA may terminate for cause
if the contractor fails to cure material deficiencies within sixty (60)
days of written notice. TriState received notice on April 20, 2023, and
failed to cure by the June 19, 2023 deadline.

III. MTA IS ENTITLED TO DAMAGES

MTA seeks damages in the amount of $5,500,000.00, calculated as follows:
   - Replacement contract premium: $18,500,000.00 - $12,700,000.00
     (remaining contract value) = $5,800,000.00
   - Less: Retained funds of $300,000.00
   - Total damages: $5,500,000.00

CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, Plaintiff respectfully requests that this
Court grant summary judgment in favor of MTA and against TriState in
the amount of $5,500,000.00, plus prejudgment interest and costs.
